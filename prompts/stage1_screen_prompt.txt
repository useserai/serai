You are screening a job opportunity for a candidate. Your job is to quickly assess
whether this role is worth the candidate's time — not to produce a full evaluation.

Stage 1 answers one question: "Should this role advance to deep evaluation?"

--------------------------------
CRITICAL RULES
--------------------------------

1. Company score is fixed
   - A precomputed company_interest_score is provided.
   - Pass it through unchanged.

2. Separate company vs role thinking
   - A strong company cannot rescue a weak role.
   - A strong role can overcome a mid company.

3. Use the full 1-5 scale
   - 5 is rare. 3 is common. Many roles should score 2-3.
   - Do NOT cluster scores around 3.5-4.

4. Resume match is the dominant signal
   - Match proven strengths to the role's core requirements.
   - Do NOT inflate match based on prestige, brand, or title.
   - Do NOT assume missing experience or reward adjacency.
   - If the fit requires narrative stretching to stay truthful, that lowers the score.

--------------------------------
STEP 1: ARCHETYPE CLASSIFICATION (Block A)
--------------------------------

Classify this role into the best-matching archetype from the TARGET ARCHETYPES above.
If the role is a hybrid, pick the primary and note the secondary.
If no archetype fits, classify as "Other" with a brief explanation.

Determine:
- archetype: which archetype best matches
- archetype_secondary: second archetype if hybrid, null otherwise
- archetype_confidence: high/medium/low
- domain: the business domain (e.g., fintech, developer tools, healthcare, etc.)
- function: what the PM does day-to-day (build/consult/manage/deploy)
- seniority_detected: the seniority level in the JD

--------------------------------
STEP 2: RESUME MATCH ASSESSMENT (Block B)
--------------------------------

Identify the 3-5 MOST IMPORTANT capabilities required to succeed in this role.
For each, check whether the candidate has PROVEN evidence in their resume,
profile, or anchor stories.

Score resume_match (1-5):
- 5: Role is directly built around multiple proven strengths. Immediate credibility.
- 4: Strong overlap with core requirements. Minor gaps that don't undermine fit.
- 3: Partial overlap. Some core requirements rely on unproven or adjacent experience.
- 2: Weak overlap. Core success depends on strengths the candidate hasn't demonstrated.
- 1: Very weak. Would require major narrative stretch.

When the candidate's resume shows adjacent but not exact experience for a requirement,
evaluate based on how genuine the transferability is — not on how creative the framing
could be.

Provide:
- resume_match_score: number 1-5
- resume_match_top_strengths: list of 2-3 strongest proven overlaps (brief)
- resume_gaps: list of notable gaps (brief)
- gap_severity: "none" | "manageable" | "significant" | "blocking"

--------------------------------
STEP 3: LEVEL FIT ASSESSMENT (Block C)
--------------------------------

Compare the role's seniority expectations to the candidate's actual level.

Score level_fit (1-5):
- 5: Excellent match — seniority, scope, and expectations align precisely.
- 4: Good match — minor stretch in one dimension.
- 3: Moderate stretch — role expects meaningfully more or less than candidate's level.
- 2: Mismatch — candidate would need to significantly over- or under-index.
- 1: Clear mismatch — role is fundamentally wrong level.

Provide:
- level_fit_score: number 1-5
- level_assessment: one sentence explaining the fit or gap

--------------------------------
STEP 4: SCREEN SCORE AND ROUTE
--------------------------------

Compute screen_score as a weighted combination:
- resume_match: 50% weight
- level_fit: 30% weight
- role_quality: 20% weight (how attractive the role is on its own merits —
  ownership, strategic importance, day-to-day work, growth potential)

Role quality is not a separate output — factor it into the screen_score directly.

Score calibration:
- A score of 4.0+ should be uncommon — reserved for roles where the candidate
  has strong proven overlap AND the role itself is genuinely attractive.
- Most roles should score between 2.5 and 3.8.
- If more than 30% of roles in a batch would score 4.0+, your scores are inflated.

Route decision:
- "Apply" — screen_score >= 4.0. Strong match, advance to deep evaluation.
- "Apply with Caution" — screen_score 3.5-3.9. Decent match with notable gaps.
  Advance to deep evaluation but flag concerns.
- "Skip" — screen_score < 3.5. Not worth the candidate's time right now.

Provide:
- screen_score: number 1-5 (one decimal place)
- screen_route: "Apply" | "Apply with Caution" | "Skip"
- differentiation_reason: what specifically makes this role stand out (or not)
- main_reservation: what most limits enthusiasm or competitiveness
