## Archetype Deep-Dive

### Strong Fit: Growth PM

Companies where growth is a first-class product function, not a marketing team side project. PMs own activation, conversion, retention, or monetization with real experimentation infrastructure. Examples: Figma (PLG motion, viral loops), Notion (self-serve onboarding at scale), Ramp (conversion optimization). What to look for: dedicated growth team, A/B testing infrastructure, product-led metrics (not just MQLs).

### Strong Fit: Platform PM

Companies building developer platforms, ecosystems, or marketplaces where PM owns the integration and adoption experience. Examples: Stripe (developer experience), Twilio (API platform), Atlassian (marketplace). Look for: API-first product, developer community, integration ecosystem as a moat.

### Moderate Fit: AI PM

Interesting if AI is the product experience, not just internal tooling. Strong fit if the company applies AI to growth or product personalization. Examples: Harvey (AI-native product), Ramp (AI for spend management). Skip if the PM role is really "AI project manager" with no product authority.

### Moderate Fit: Core Product PM

General product management at a strong company. Only attractive if the company has exceptional product culture and the role has clear ownership. Skip if PM is a ticket-writing function.

### Weak Fit: Adjacent Roles

TPM, product ops, solutions — only worth considering at exceptional companies where the role has strategic scope, not just coordination.

## Dimension Anchors with Context

### Moat Durability (weight: 0.25 — highest priority)

A company with structural advantages gives PMs more leverage. You're not fighting commoditization — you're optimizing a machine that already works.

**9-10 (Strong YES):** Atlassian (workflow lock-in + marketplace), Figma (collaboration + design system lock-in), Stripe (payments network effects + developer ecosystem).

**7-8 (YES):** Notion (switching costs, templates, team data), Linear (developer workflow depth).

**5-6 (MAYBE):** Moderate moat — competitive but defensible niche.

**3-4 (NO):** Easily replicated feature set, no switching costs, competing on price.

**Red flag:** Company's pitch is "like X but cheaper" or "AI-powered Y" with no structural advantage.

### Growth Trajectory (weight: 0.20)

Growth = interesting PM problems. Stagnation = optimizing a shrinking pie.

**9-10:** Hypergrowth (3x+ YoY) — category-defining momentum, new segments opening.

**7-8:** Strong growth (~2x YoY) — hiring aggressively, shipping fast, clear expansion path.

**Red flag:** "Steady state" or declining, hiring freeze, growth team being cut.

### Product Culture (weight: 0.20)

This is the difference between doing PM work and pretending to. Look for direct evidence, not inference from revenue.

**9-10 (Strong YES):** Linear (PMs write specs, own outcomes, ship weekly), Stripe (writing culture, product rigor), Figma (design-led product decisions, PMs have authority).

**7-8 (YES):** Product-first leadership, PMs influence strategy, experimentation is real.

**5-6 (MAYBE):** Some product influence, but sales or eng often overrides PM decisions.

**Red flag:** "Product manager" title but the role is writing Jira tickets from sales requests. No CPO or product-first founder. PM reports to engineering or sales.

### AI Leverage (weight: 0.15)

Is AI a meaningful force multiplier on the product experience, or just a feature checkbox?

**9-10:** Harvey (AI-native product in specialized domain), Anthropic (AI is the product).

**7-8:** Ramp (AI improves core spend management), Notion AI (AI enhances existing workflow).

**Red flag:** "We use AI" on the careers page but no AI in the product. ChatGPT wrapper with no proprietary data advantage.

### Leadership Quality (weight: 0.10)

Founders with product taste and exec hires that signal product investment.

**9-10:** Product-first founders with prior exits, CPO with strong track record.

**7-8:** Credible leadership, product has a seat at the exec table.

**Red flag:** CEO is from finance/sales background, no CPO, VP Product is third in 18 months, public statements don't match product reality.

### Market Category (weight: 0.10)

Category tailwinds sustain PM investment. Shrinking categories cut growth teams first.

**9-10:** AI applications, fintech, developer tools — expanding TAM.

**7-8:** Security, vertical SaaS — healthy growth with real workflow pain.

**Red flag:** Declining category, consolidation risk, "everyone has this now."

## Disqualifier Definitions

**Consultancy / Agency**

Company's primary business is building software for *other* companies, not owning and operating its own product. Example: Accenture, McKinsey Digital, Deloitte Consulting. Even with "product manager" titles, you're staffed onto client projects — no long-term ownership, no compounding.

**Legacy Enterprise Sales Org**

Large established company where the sales organization drives product decisions and PM has no meaningful ownership. Example: Oracle, SAP, Salesforce. PM writes specs for what sales already promised. No experimentation culture, no PLG motion, no real product authority.

**Consumer Social / Gaming**

Company's primary business is consumer-facing social or gaming. Growth mechanics are interesting (viral loops, engagement) but domain mismatch for B2B product management. Skip unless the role is specifically in a B2B or platform division.

## Role-Level Criteria

When evaluating a specific role posting, screen for:

**Must-haves:**
- San Francisco Bay Area or Remote
- Total comp $250K+ (senior/staff level)
- Growth, monetization, or activation explicitly in scope
- PM owns outcomes, not just ships features

**Strong signals:**
- Experimentation infrastructure or A/B testing mentioned
- Ownership of conversion funnel, onboarding, or trial optimization
- Cross-functional collaboration with data science and engineering
- Product-led growth motion (not purely sales-assisted)
- Small, high-leverage team with direct impact on revenue
- Company publishes product or growth blog

**Red flags:**
- "Growth marketing" title with no product ownership
- Pure analytics or data science role without product authority
- Platform or infrastructure PM with no user-facing scope
- Roles where "growth" means headcount planning or team scaling
- Enterprise sales enablement disguised as product management
- Job posting recycled from last year (role not filled, might be broken)

## Interview Prep — What to Ask

When you get an interview, clarify:

1. "What does the growth team own end-to-end? What metrics are you accountable for?" (Look for revenue or activation metrics, not vanity metrics.)
2. "Walk me through a recent experiment — what did you test, what did you learn?" (Shows whether experimentation is real or aspirational.)
3. "How does the growth team interact with core product and engineering?" (Reveals whether growth is embedded or siloed.)
4. "What's your current experimentation velocity — how many tests per month?" (Low velocity = no real experimentation culture.)
5. "What's the biggest growth lever you haven't been able to pull yet, and why?" (Candor about constraints is a good sign.)
